The method

The Debt Freedom System

Most families in debt don't have a spending problem first — they have a visibility problem. This system takes a household from "we owe… a lot, probably" to a dated, month-by-month path to zero — and then flips the same payment habit into savings and ownership.

Phase 1 — See It All (the full ledger)

Rule: no plan is built from memory. Documents only. We collect both partners' credit reports, every loan statement, and the household's own list — the reports always catch things the list misses. Everything goes into one ledger: every account, owner, balance, payment, and rate, each figure tagged confirmed or estimated, each counted exactly once. In the founding household, the family's own list had missed 29 buy-now-pay-later loans and an entire credit card.

Deliverable: a ledger where the total is a sum you watched being built.

Phase 2 — Sort the Piles

Not everything on a credit report is yours to pay. The $0 pile: authorized-user accounts that belong to someone else, charge-offs you're not liable for or are disputing, fraudulent accounts — these get removal actions and a re-pull date 30–45 days out to prove deletion, never a payment. The pay pile is everything else, including the uncomfortable items. In the founding household the $0 pile was over $53,000 — sorting the piles is the single highest-value hour in the system.

Deliverable: both piles, itemized, with a removal action and date for every $0-pile line.

Phase 3 — Stop the Leaks

Before any extra dollar goes to debt: everything current and on autopay (the founding household burned $207 in late fees in a single month on two vehicle accounts). Cards being paid off leave wallets and saved checkouts; buy-now-pay-later is banned outright. Vehicles are never surrendered or rolled into new loans while underwater. And every deferred-interest promo gets a calendar date — a balance is either paid before its promo ends or moved to the front of the line.

Deliverable: the leak list, closed, with promo-cliff dates on the calendar.

Phase 4 — Pick Your Speed

We simulate your household month by month at three total monthly budgets — Good, Better, Best — next to the minimums-only baseline, so you see the real cost of doing nothing. The method is a lump-sum avalanche roll: all minimums paid, every spare dollar to the highest-rate target, and when a debt dies its payment rolls to the next. The total payment never steps down. For each speed you get a debt-free date, total interest, and milestone dates — then you pick, together, with your eyes open.

Deliverable: three modeled speeds and your chosen one, dated.

Phase 5 — Run It (the monthly rhythm)

Day 1: fixed lines verified current and on autopay, first attack payments scheduled. Every month after: a 15-minute review of actual balances against the model's schedule — ahead, on, or behind, we adjust calmly and never abandon. Milestones are marked on schedule (first account zero, cards zero, halfway, break-even on each vehicle), and removals are verified on re-pulls as the plan runs.

Deliverable: the monthly review rhythm and your milestone calendar.

Phase 6 — Flip It (from payoff to ownership)

The month after debt-free, the same payment — never reduced — flips to savings: a $1,000 starter buffer, then $25,000, then a $75,000 war chest that's starter-deal ready for a first business. Debt was the training ground; ownership is the point.

Deliverable: the war-chest ladder, dated from your debt-free month.

The house rules

Every family in the system runs on the same eight rules: one war payment every month, never stepping down · autopay everything · no new debt until free · lump sums go 100% to the current target the day they land · fixed lines stay current and untouched · never sell a vehicle underwater · removals are tracked, not paid · review monthly, adjust calmly, never abandon.

Honesty standards

Every figure in a plan traces to a document or is labeled an estimate. Scenario dates are planning estimates — real rates move dates by weeks, not the shape. The system never tells a family to pay a debt they're disputing as not theirs, never recommends default, surrender, or settlement as a shortcut, and refers tax, legal, and bankruptcy questions to licensed professionals.
